Design decisions & open questions — count-fill-tenths.html

Decisions

  • The tenths control is one large continuous tap/drag surface (the vertical gauge), not ten separate 35px-wide segment buttons. Ten independent buttons across a 358px content width would each be ~35px — under the 44px tap-min floor. A single big gauge with internal gridlines gives full tenth-resolution ("a way to hit every tenth") without shipping a sub-44px tap target; FULL/HALF/EMPTY stay as three explicit tap-primary (56px+) buttons for the fast common case.
  • Two different server actions are modeled as two different UI actions, on purpose: "+ Add bottle to line" stages a brand-new observation and maps to `incrementCountLineAction`'s `newPartialFills` (an append); tapping an existing bottle chip switches to "Update / Remove" and maps to `editCountLineFillsAction`, which replaces the whole `partialFills` array. The UI has to know which one it's calling based on whether the bottle already exists server-side — see the report back for why this was awkward.
  • The keg example reuses the identical gauge/buttons/list mechanism as the bottle example — per CLAUDE.md, a tapped keg is "no special-casing," just `partial_fills` on a product with a bigger `size_ml`. The mL total shown for the keg (derived as fraction × size_ml) is display-only.
  • Unpriced (Coors Light keg has no cost on file) collapses the value row to one column exactly like the scan screen, with an owner-only "no cost on file" note — never `$0.00`.

Open questions for the owner

  • Should removing a bottle from `partial_fills` require a confirmation step? Right now it's a single tap on the X — fast, but a fat thumb could delete a real observation with no undo.
  • Is there ever a reason to see a specific bottle's fill *history* (was it 0.3 an hour ago, corrected to 0.4 now)? `editCountLineFillsAction` doesn't currently write to the `count_line_write` ledger (see lib/domain/counts.ts's comment on that function) — so today a fill correction leaves no audit trail the way a quantity correction does. Worth deciding if that's acceptable before this ships.
